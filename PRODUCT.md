# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

delegated: plain static HTML/CSS/JS, no build step, so it can be pushed to a Git repo and served by GitHub Pages as-is.

## Users

AP Calculus AB students in one class (2026–27, period 1), studying for the Unit 4 test on Tue Nov 10. Used on phones between classes and on laptops at home while doing homework.

## Product Purpose

One place for the whole class to study Unit 4: the day-by-day schedule, each lesson's key notes, worked examples, links to the teacher's slides, homework and solutions on Canvas, and the interactive related-rates sheets. Success: a student can find "what did we do on Day X and how do I do this kind of problem" in seconds.

## Positioning

Student-made companion to the Canvas page. Canvas has the files; this site has the explanations plus seven hands-on simulations (shrinking cylinder, two cars, soap bubble, draining tank, Velocity Raptor, evaporating cone, water in and out).

## Operating Context

Source of truth for dates and files is the teacher's Canvas page (knoxschools.instructure.com, course 1480483). Canvas file links require a student login. Slides are Google Slides. Calculator use is expected.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Static only, no accounts, no server.
- Unit 4 learning objectives: CHA-3.A–F, LIM-4.A.
- Day 6 and Day 7 slides and solutions were not yet posted (as of Nov 3); show them as "not posted yet", never invent links.
- Inferred (not confirmed by user): no teacher branding or school logo should be used.

## Evidence on Hand

- `labs/` — the existing interactive related-rates page and its images (user's own work).
- Notes and Check-Your-Understanding examples read from the teacher's slide decks for Days 1–5.

## Product Principles

1. Find it fast: schedule and topic lookup beat decoration.
2. Explain, then link: every day has a short "what you need to know" before the files.
3. Show the math the way the class writes it (dV/dt notation, units on answers, round to 3 decimals).
4. Works on a phone.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Readable math without a math-rendering library; good contrast in light and dark; keyboard-usable.
